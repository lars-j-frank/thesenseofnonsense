### Newsroom lead

Four fiscal years of audited TIER Fund statements show $590.0 million in innovation and technology grants and $1,088.8 million in transfers to Alberta's General Revenue Fund. That works out to about $1.85 transferred for every dollar granted. The fund also reported a $1.105 billion accumulated surplus at 31 March 2025.

The calculation uses the TIER Fund Statements of Operations for fiscal 2022 through fiscal 2025. The legal authority for transfers to general revenue is public. The reporting question is what those transferred dollars funded after they entered the government's general accounts.

## Why the series matters

Most public discussion of TIER concerns the carbon price, credit supply, industrial competitiveness, or the projects named in funding announcements. The TIER Files reads the financial statements as a continuous record.

That record permits several questions to be answered with numbers:

* How much compliance revenue entered the fund?
* How much left as innovation and technology grants?
* How much moved to general revenue?
* How much remained as accumulated surplus?
* How much cash and investment capital did ERA hold?
* How much committed funding reached projects as milestone payments?
* What happened when contribution agreements were cancelled or terminated?
* What do the related-party notes disclose about management-owned companies?

The series treats legality, financial disclosure, and public accountability as separate questions. Every major claim links to the underlying public record.

## Verified findings

### 1. Four years of TIER Fund cash flow

For the fiscal years ended 31 March 2022 through 31 March 2025:

| Audited line item | Four-year total |
|---|---:|
| TIER Fund revenue | $2,641.1 million |
| Innovation and technology grants | $590.0 million |
| Transfers to the General Revenue Fund | $1,088.8 million |

The transfer-to-grant ratio is 1.845 to 1, rounded to **$1.85 transferred for every dollar granted**.

The TIER Fund reported an accumulated surplus of **$1,105.4 million at 31 March 2025**, up from $336.8 million at 31 March 2022. Accumulated surplus is a balance-sheet stock. Grants and transfers in the table above are four-year flows.

**Primary record:** Government of Alberta, *Environment and Protected Areas Annual Report 2024-2025*, TIER Fund audited financial statements, Statement of Operations, p. 56; prior-year comparative statements in the 2023-2024 and 2022-2023 annual reports.

**Careful wording:** The statements establish the transfers. Tracing those dollars into later government expenditures would require a separate reconciliation.

### 2. ERA's cash and investments

At 31 May 2025, ERA reported:

* cash of **$309.5 million**;
* short-term investments of **$40.0 million**; and
* long-term investments of **$190.0 million**.

The combined balance was **$539.5 million**. ERA reported **$71.7 million** in project expenses for fiscal 2025. The balance was therefore about 7.5 times that year's project expense. This is a comparison of a point-in-time balance with one year's expense, rather than a liquidity forecast.

ERA also reported **$25.6 million in interest income** in fiscal 2025. Across fiscal 2021 through fiscal 2025, the statements report about **$84.4 million** in interest income.

**Primary record:** Emissions Reduction Alberta, audited financial statements for the year ended 31 May 2025, Statement of Financial Position and Notes 3 and 5; prior annual statements for the five-year interest series.

### 3. The 2024 allocation and interest reduction

ERA's fiscal 2025 statements describe a provincial allocation letter dated 26 August 2024. The allocation was $50.0 million. It was reduced by $29.7 million, equal to ERA's fiscal 2024 interest income, leaving a cash transfer of about $20.3 million. An amendment dated 11 October 2024 added a continuing mechanism for reducing provincial payments by interest earned on government funding.

**Primary record:** ERA audited financial statements for the year ended 31 May 2025, Note 3.

**Careful wording:** “Interest reduction” or “interest netting” tracks the financial statement. “Clawback” is the series' characterization.

### 4. Agreements cancelled or terminated

ERA reported **$82,737,012** in contribution agreements cancelled or terminated during fiscal 2025. It reported **$71.7 million** in project expenses for the same year.

The two amounts measure different things. The first is the value removed from contribution agreements. The second is project expense recognized during the year. The comparison shows that the value cancelled or terminated exceeded project expense in fiscal 2025. The $82.7 million is agreement value removed, rather than cash paid and recovered. Milestone funding generally remains with ERA until earned.

**Primary record:** ERA audited financial statements for the year ended 31 May 2025, Note 10 and Statement of Operations.

### 5. Lifetime commitments and cash paid to projects

Float reconstructs about **$650 million** in cumulative project expense from ERA and predecessor CCEMC statements covering fiscal 2010 through fiscal 2025. ERA's public material in June 2026 said it had committed **$1.17 billion toward 352 projects** since 2009.

The resulting lifetime paid-to-committed comparison is about **55 to 60 cents per committed dollar**.

**Qualification required:** This is the series' reconstruction. The fiscal 2015 annual report was published without financial statements, so the lifetime project-expense total includes an estimate for that year. “About 55 to 60 cents” is the approved wording.

### 6. Remuneration paid to management-owned companies

ERA's audited statements say that program management expenses include remuneration to **“companies owned by senior management who report directly to the board.”** The statements report **$321,613** in such fees for fiscal 2025 and **$304,999** for fiscal 2024.

The series reconstructs about **$2.75 million** in the related remuneration line across fiscal 2017 through fiscal 2025.

The fiscal 2017 comparative also changed from **$4,383,880** in the fiscal 2017 statements to **$323,085** in the fiscal 2018 statements, a reduction of about 93 per cent. Explaining the change would require a reconciliation beyond the public financial statements reviewed for the series.

**Primary record:** ERA audited financial statements, fiscal 2017 through fiscal 2025; fiscal 2025 related-party disclosure at p. 12.

**Careful wording:** The transactions are disclosed in audited statements. Company names and owner identities would require additional records. The series documents relationships and leaves influence over funding decisions as a separate evidentiary question.

### 7. Alberta and British Columbia RNG attributes

Part 5 follows publicly funded Alberta biogas projects into Alberta's offset registry and British Columbia Utilities Commission records for biomethane purchases. Alberta's 2023 *Emissions Reduction and Energy Development Plan* states that most Alberta bioenergy, particularly renewable natural gas, is shipped to British Columbia.

The public records establish overlapping funding, registry, and biomethane-purchase systems. A specific serialized double count remains unproven.

**Approved wording:** The public record shows a risk of overlapping claims. Proving a specific overlap would require a serial-to-gigajoule reconciliation.

**Avoid:** “The series proved double counting.”

## Reporter questions

### Is the transfer of TIER money to general revenue legal?

Yes. Alberta law permits qualifying TIER Fund money to be transferred to the General Revenue Fund. The finding is the amount transferred. Tracing those dollars into later spending would require a separate published reconciliation.

### What does “committed” mean?

ERA contribution agreements generally pay against milestones. The audited statements distinguish project expense from outstanding commitments. The series uses “paid,” “project expense,” and “committed” according to those separate records.

### What happens to the cancelled value?

Where a project has yet to earn a milestone payment, ERA retains the cash. Cancellation removes the remaining commitment and returns that capacity to the available pool.

### What did Part 5 establish about double counting?

It documented systems capable of producing overlapping claims. A specific double count remains unproven and would require evidence tying Alberta offset serials to the gigajoules sold under the BCUC records.

### How are the management-owned-company payments disclosed?

They are disclosed annually in ERA's audited statements. The note gives an amount and a category. Company names, owner identities, purchased services, and procurement details would require additional records.

### When is the next TIER statutory review due?

Order in Council 369/2025 amended section 39 of the TIER Regulation and moved the review deadline from 31 December 2026 to 31 December 2030. Earlier government fact sheets carrying the 2026 date predate that amendment.

## Correction log

### 17 July 2026

Earlier series planning referred to a statutory TIER review due by 31 December 2026. Order in Council 369/2025 had already moved the regulatory review deadline to 31 December 2030. The series landing page, Part 2, and Part 7 were corrected. The change does not affect the financial findings.

The five-year ERA interest total in Parts 3 and 4 was corrected from $104.4 million to $84.4 million. The original annual figures were accurate; their displayed sum was not. The press kit uses the corrected total.

Part 1 no longer says general-revenue transfers exceeded grants in every year. The audited Statements of Operations show that pattern in fiscal 2022 through 2024 only; in fiscal 2025 grants were $109.4 million and transfers $24.7 million. The four-year $1.85 ratio is unchanged.

Part 2 now matches the Auditor General December 2023 recommendation table: 19 outstanding recommendations at Environment and Protected Areas, of which 11 were older than three years.
