Alberta's Technology Innovation and Emissions Reduction (TIER) system has been operating since 2020, collecting a price on carbon from the province's largest industrial emitters. The system was designed with a clear purpose: create a financial incentive for emissions reduction and reinvest the proceeds into clean technology.

But the public financial statements tell a more complicated story.

## The arithmetic

Between 2020 and 2024, TIER collected approximately $2.6 billion from regulated emitters. What happened to that money is documented in the Alberta government's annual public accounts and the TIER fund administrator's financial reports.

The line items are all there. You just have to know where to look.

<section class="prose-callout">

**Key finding:** Of the $2.6 billion collected, less than half was disbursed for programs directly connected to emissions reduction. A significant portion moved through the TIER fund into general government revenue, allocated to programs with no measurable connection to greenhouse gas abatement.

</section>

## The architecture

TIER works as an output-based pricing system. Large facilities pay on the portion of their emissions above a facility-specific benchmark. They can also meet their compliance obligation by purchasing Alberta-based carbon credits or by contributing to the TIER fund — the "funding option" that generates the $600M+ annual inflow.

The money goes into the Climate Change and Emissions Management Fund, administered by the Climate Change and Emissions Management Corporation. The corporation's mandate: invest in technology and research that reduces emissions.

The question is whether the mandate matches the outcome.
